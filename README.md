# AI-Augmented Canonical Scripture Study

An open-source project being built to support careful study of the Bible in its original languages and wider biblical context. It aims to help people investigate Scripture with AI assistance, keeping source evidence distinct from interpretation and conclusions.

The application is one practical means of supporting the Vision and Mission below. It is currently in early development.

The Vision, Mission and Principles below reproduce **version 0.10** of the project foundation.

## Vision

**To grow in knowing God as He reveals Himself through Scripture, deepening love for Him and for others, and responding with faith, trust and obedience.**

Jesus teaches that loving God and loving our neighbor are the greatest commandments (Mark 12:28–34).

Knowing God is more than gaining knowledge. It should shape how we relate to Him and how we live.

Scripture helps us understand what God has revealed about Himself, His character, His purposes and His will.

Growing in this understanding is a continuing journey. Human knowledge and interpretation remain incomplete and must be open to correction.

The desired outcome is not simply greater biblical knowledge, but lives increasingly shaped by knowing, loving and following God.

## Mission

**To seek a faithful understanding of Scripture through careful, honest and thorough study, allowing God's Word to shape understanding, challenge assumptions and guide how we live.**

This means studying Scripture in its original and wider contexts, seeking to understand what its writers communicated and how the biblical writings fit together.

It means examining interpretations carefully, considering relevant evidence and alternative explanations, and being willing to correct mistaken beliefs rather than simply defending familiar ones.

The aim is to understand what God has revealed as faithfully as possible, recognize the limits of human understanding and continue learning.

**Studying Scripture is not merely an intellectual exercise. Its purpose is to deepen knowledge of God and help put that understanding into practice.**

## Principles

These principles guide the pursuit of the Mission and the work undertaken to support it, including studying Scripture, designing, building and maintaining the application.

They do not replace Scripture or attempt to define every aspect of theology. They describe the commitments that should guide decisions.

### Theological Foundations

**P-01 — Love God and love others**

Love for God and for others is our highest aim. Growing in knowledge of God should strengthen our love, trust and obedience, and shape how we treat others.

**P-02 — God's authority and Scripture**

God is the ultimate authority. Scripture is the authoritative foundation for understanding what He has revealed about Himself, His purposes and His will.

**P-03 — Christ is central to Scripture**

Jesus Christ is central to God's revelation. The Old and New Testaments together reveal God's purposes, which find their fulfillment in Christ.

**P-04 — God's revealed character**

God's character, as revealed in Scripture, is the standard against which our understanding of Him and our theological conclusions must be evaluated. Our descriptions of His character, including accepted teachings, are themselves fallible and remain subject to Scripture.

**P-05 — Humility and willingness to be corrected**

Studying Scripture requires humility, recognizing that our understanding is fallible, even when our beliefs are deeply held or widely accepted.

We must approach Scripture with a willingness to learn, allowing it to shape us, challenge our assumptions and change our understanding and beliefs.

**P-06 — Prayer and the Holy Spirit**

Faithful study of Scripture requires prayer and dependence on the Holy Spirit. We seek His guidance to understand God's Word, grow in truth, and allow what we learn to transform how we live.

### How We Interpret Scripture

**P-07 — Understand Scripture in its own context**

Scripture should be understood in its own context. Every passage is part of a larger work, written in a particular language, literary form, historical period and cultural setting.

Understanding a passage requires considering the surrounding text, the purpose of the writing, its original audience, the historical and cultural circumstances in which it was written, and how it fits within the work as a whole.

**P-08 — Study Scripture in its original languages**

Scripture should be studied with attention to its original languages—Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek. The wording, grammar and syntax of the original texts help us understand what the biblical writers communicated and evaluate the choices made in translation.

**P-09 — Respect Scripture's literary forms**

Scripture contains different kinds of writing, including narrative, poetry, wisdom, prophecy and letters. Each communicates in its own way and should be understood accordingly, including its use of imagery, symbolism and other literary devices.

**P-10 — Let Scripture help explain Scripture**

Scripture should be understood as a unified whole, where passages can illuminate one another. Each passage must still be understood in its own context, without forcing them to say the same thing. Later revelation can deepen our understanding of earlier passages without changing their original meaning.

**P-11 — Respect the distinct biblical witnesses**

Each biblical author writes from a distinct context and purpose. When passages describe related events or teachings, we should respect each account's unique contribution, including meaningful differences.

**P-12 — Distinguish recorded words from biblical teaching**

Scripture can report someone’s words without confirming that what they said is true. We should distinguish what a passage records from what it teaches.

**P-13 — Respect the available textual evidence**

We must distinguish between available manuscript evidence, editorial reconstructions, and interpretations. Textual uncertainties or differences should be acknowledged.

### Study, Research and Honesty

**P-14 — Seek to learn from Scripture**

Studying Scripture seeks to understand what it actually teaches. We approach it to learn rather than to confirm preconceived notions.

**P-15 — Evaluate other sources against Scripture**

Other sources can help us investigate and understand Scripture, but they cannot determine what Scripture teaches.

**P-16 — Use sound reasoning**

Interpretations must be supported by Scripture in context and by sound reasoning. Arguments must avoid circular reasoning and unsupported assumptions.

**P-17 — Keep evidence and interpretation distinct**

We must distinguish between what Scripture actually states and what we conclude from it. Interpretations must not be confused with the original evidence.

**P-18 — Study with care and focus**

We study with care and focus. When a question is significant, we go deeper. When it's simple, we keep it simple.

**P-19 — Honesty about conclusions and uncertainty**

Conclusions should reflect the strength of the available evidence. We should be honest about what is known, what remains uncertain, and what the evidence does not support.

Uncertainty should be acknowledged where it exists, without avoiding conclusions that are supported by sufficient evidence.

### Ethics, Responsibility and Stewardship

**P-20 — Act with integrity**

We act honestly and responsibly, respecting applicable laws, intellectual property and agreements.

We respect both the letter and the spirit of these obligations, rather than looking for ways around them.

**P-21 — Respect privacy**

We respect people's privacy and handle personal and sensitive information with care.

Information entrusted to us should only be used or shared for its intended and authorized purpose.

**P-22 — Personal responsibility**

We are responsible for how we use our knowledge, beliefs, abilities and the resources available to us.

We should exercise good judgment, act with integrity and use what has been entrusted to us for good rather than misuse it.

### Quality and Simplicity

**P-23 — Quality and Simplicity**

We aim for quality in everything we do, using the simplest approach that fulfills its purpose well.

Complexity should only be introduced when it is necessary to achieve the intended result, maintain quality or address a real risk.

Quality is demonstrated by how well the result serves its purpose in practice.
